Every RAG demo is a single question. Someone types "how do I reset the gateway credentials", the retriever pulls the right three chunks, the model answers, everyone nods.

Then it goes to real users and the second turn looks like this:

```
User: how do I reset the gateway credentials
Bot:  [correct answer]
User: and for the staging one?
Bot:  I don't have information about that.
```

Nothing is broken. The retriever did exactly what you asked. You handed it the string `and for the staging one?` and asked for the nearest chunks in embedding space. That string is about nothing. Its vector sits in a bland region of the space near other short fragments, nowhere near your gateway documentation. You get back three useless chunks and the model, correctly, refuses to invent an answer.

I hit this on a support-facing pipeline at Amdocs and spent longer than I'd like to admit looking at the wrong layer.

## The fixes that don't work

**Stuffing the history into the query.** The obvious move is to concatenate the last few turns and embed the whole thing. This does put the word "gateway" back in the query, so it looks like progress. But you have also embedded the previous answer, the greeting, and whatever else was in the buffer. The resulting vector is an average of everything the conversation has touched, which means it points at the centroid of the conversation rather than at what the user just asked. Retrieval gets vaguer with every turn.

**Raising top-k.** If the right chunk isn't in the top 3, surely it's in the top 20. Sometimes it is. You've also handed the model 17 irrelevant chunks, and a model given mostly-noise context gets worse at saying "I don't know". This trades a visible failure for a hallucination, which is a bad trade.

**A better model.** The generator was never the problem. It answered correctly given what it received. No model recovers from context that doesn't contain the answer.

All three miss the same thing: the failure happened before retrieval, in the query itself.

## Rewrite the question first

Put a step in front of the retriever whose only job is to turn a context-dependent question into a standalone one. Give it the recent history and the new message, and ask for a self-contained version.

```python
REWRITE_PROMPT = """Given the conversation, rewrite the user's last message
as a standalone question that makes sense without the conversation.
Resolve pronouns and references. Do not answer it.
If it is already standalone, return it unchanged.

Conversation:
{history}

Last message: {question}
Standalone question:"""

async def retrieve(question: str, history: list[Turn]) -> list[Chunk]:
    search_query = question
    if history:
        search_query = await llm.complete(
            REWRITE_PROMPT.format(history=render(history[-4:]), question=question)
        )
    return await vectorstore.similarity_search(search_query, k=5)
```

`and for the staging one?` becomes `how do I reset the gateway credentials for the staging environment?`. That embeds to something meaningful, and retrieval starts working again.

The rewritten query goes to the retriever only. The generator still sees the user's original words and the real history, because "and for the staging one?" carries a tone and a brevity that you don't want to launder through a rewrite before answering it.

## The details that decide whether it works

**Skip it on the first turn.** No history means nothing to resolve, and running the rewrite anyway just adds latency and a chance to mangle a perfectly good question.

**Cap the history.** Four turns was enough for us. Feed it twenty and the rewriter starts pulling in stale topics, which reintroduces the exact drift you added it to fix.

**Tell it not to answer.** Without that line in the prompt, a capable model will helpfully answer the question instead of rewriting it, and you'll embed the answer. This one is easy to miss because it fails silently — you get a plausible string back either way.

**Keep the original when the rewrite is empty or absurd.** Cheap guardrail, saves you from a bad generation nuking retrieval entirely.

**Budget the latency.** This is a second model call on the critical path. A small fast model handles it fine; we ran it on the cheapest one available and it cost roughly 300ms. Whether that's acceptable depends on your product, but it's a real cost and you should measure it rather than assume.

## How to know it's actually working

The reason this bug survives to production is that nobody tests for it. Standard RAG eval sets are lists of independent questions, and the pipeline passes every one of them.

Build a second eval set made only of multi-turn sequences: an opening question, then two or three follow-ups that lean on pronouns and ellipsis. Score retrieval on the follow-up turns specifically. Ours went from a majority of follow-ups retrieving nothing relevant to most of them landing the right chunk, and the number that mattered to the business — support tickets escalated after the bot gave up — moved with it.

If your eval set has no follow-ups in it, you don't yet know whether you have this bug. You probably do.
